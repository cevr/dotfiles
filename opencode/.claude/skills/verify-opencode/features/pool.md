# Account pool

The pool chooses the login each request of a pooled provider (Claude) goes out on: a session stays on its login while its prompt cache lives, a login the provider refuses (a plan limit, a lost token) is held and the refused request goes out at once on another, a reset window frees a held login, and the login store's lost logins and failing refreshes surface as alerts. Other mods read it over the `dotfiles-opencode.pool` RPC: orchestrate's pace, the `/pool` pane, the sidebar's login label.

## Sub-features

- `pool-choose`: a run goes out on a login with room; a session with a live cache stays on its login.
- `pool-failover`: a plan-limit 429 holds the login and the run finishes on the next.
- `pool-reset`: a held login is used again after its reset.
- `pool-claim`: the pool claims a usage-limit reset on its own (`claim-rule.ts`): one about to expire (in its last day, on anything it gives back), else one when the forecast of the work running across the fleet (its burn, the sessions busy now on every host however long ago they started, any start held) runs the pool out before its first natural reset, on the most-used account near its limit; it never asks, and tells the owner after with one `OK` notice in the waiting session's tray.
- `pool-store`: the login store's accounts, read and refreshed in place under its lock.
- `pool-alerts`: a lost login or failing refreshes name the slot file to sign in again.
- `pool-rpc`: `pace`, `status`, `session`, `alerts` answer the other mods; `pace` with `dispatch: true` answers as a request's dispatch, with the logins it may go out on (`eligible`).
- `pool-admission`: every request of a pooled provider is admitted at its dispatch by the one pace a start gets (`place`): a fresh or cached root's, a child's, a retry, a follow-up, the cache's keepalive ping. Over the spend-evenly line it goes out on no login (`RequestHeld`, with why and when it opens) unless a reset the pool claimed, sent once and observed lower in a fresh reading of the same week, rebased the line (`admission: "backed"`); a reset in hand adds no room. Only a claim answered `reset` gives room (an `already-redeemed` answer gives none, however low the week reads); a claim whose reading after it failed, or read the week unchanged (the provider's reporting lags), stays pending (`claim pending` in the held answer; its account settling, so no second reset is claimed) until an own reading of the same week within 30 minutes of the claim shows the week lower, which is its observation; it is never sent again, and past the 30 minutes, or once the week rolled over, it gives no room. The pace counts only readings of the last 30 minutes whose week resets within the next 7 days, and for a model with a window of its own (`7d:Fable`) that window read as freshly on the login: a dispatch reads a login with a stale one once more, a login lacking one or with a week no period can have takes no request of it, and with no such reading on any login the request goes out on none (held with what each lacks, again in a minute). On backed room a request goes out only on the login whose claim gave it, and only while that room still holds its share (`fleet.startCost`): it reserves the share before it goes (`resets/spend/`, kept across restarts and read back by every instance on the host), so two requests on one reading never spend one slot twice; a title or another auxiliary request reserves none and waits. Admitted, it goes out only on a login eligible for its model; the cache keeps its login only while that login is eligible.

Limits belong to the model and account, not to the last response: both the provider's defined scoped windows and any it has shown remain required through omissions, outages and rollovers. A past reset (including a 5-hour reset) calls for a new reading, not an assumption of zero use. Any directory instance can record a pending claim's recovery on the shared host ledger. Every held dispatch, including a title, names a future time to try again and is decided afresh then. The first relevant blocking reset, an ordinary catch-up, or a weekly rollover may change the answer; without fresh evidence it checks again in a minute.

A named start and a dispatch use the same rule on the same readings and launch reservations. After two distinct successful usage responses omit a required window the login has never reported, the pool says to use another model or login; the retry time is an on-demand usage refresh, not a promise the login will open. Orchestrate uses the start hold to fall back before starting an agent on the unsupported login.

## How to get to it (user POV)

- Any primary request on a pooled provider: the user's prompt, an agent's turn, a compaction.
- The `/pool` pane (the status mod) and the sidebar's login line.
- Orchestrate's pace, before each role start.

## Driving it with the world

- **Preconditions:** a world made with `anthropic` (sim/provider.ts, the fake's wire) and two or more logins; the Anthropic auth plugin and the pool loaded as the real run loads them.
- **States:** default, refused (a 429), a reset, error (the faults suite: failed store writes, a refresh outage). Loading is `n/a`.
- **World suites:** `cmd/pool/pool-reset-backed-pace.sim.test.ts`, `cmd/pool/pool-failover.sim.test.ts`, `cmd/pool/pool-resets.sim.test.ts`, `cmd/pool/pool-faults.sim.test.ts`, `cmd/pool/pool-store.sim.test.ts`, `cmd/pool/pool-store-refresh.sim.test.ts`, `cmd/pool/pool-store-opencode.sim.test.ts`, `cmd/pool/pool-waiter.sim.test.ts`
- **Code:** `mods/pool/`, `cmd/pool/`, `mods/anthropic-auth/`, `cmd/anthropic-auth/`, `sim/`, `tests/pool-resets.test.ts`, `testbeds/busy-agents.ts` (the resets' real check and the busy agents it runs); `tests/owned.ts`, `tests/scratch.ts` (the spawn helper and scratch folders its real checks run through: a change to them is due on every such check)
- **Regression:** `mods/pool/*.test.ts`.

1. **Failover.** The first login answers an agent turn with a plan-limit 429; the run finishes on the second, and the world's provider saw exactly those two requests in that order.
2. **Without the pool.** The same refusal ends the run: the host does not retry a plan limit on its own.
3. **Resets and store.** The resets suite holds a login to its reset, claims a reset on its own only with agents running (sessions busy on a long command, no start recorded) whose forecast runs the pool out before its reset (one per pool, the claimed login taking the work), leaves one while a fresher account's room outlasts the reset, uses a reset about to expire, and asks nothing; the store suites read, refresh and write the login store under its lock; a resolve that waited out another holder's lock reads the file again and serves only the login it read before the wait, unrefused, else fails over (busy).
4. **Reset-backed pace.** `pool-reset-backed-pace.sim.test.ts`: a Fable allowance at 4–8% of its own week admits nothing past the even line and claims nothing; an Opus start held by a 100% shared week claims the Fable reset once and is admitted after it; the owner's fixture (shared 100%, Fable 68% = 34 shared points) claims once, reads the week at 66% after it, admits as `backed`, a dispatch naming its one eligible login, and claims nothing more; a 0.4% Fable part claims nothing; a cached root's continued turn over the line sends no request; a natural weekly rollover reopens it with no claim. At the fake provider's bytes: two requests at one instant on one 4-point slot send one, a restart after it still holds the second; a cached login never spends another login's refund; a login unread, or read 31 minutes ago, sends nothing; an `already_used` answer while another actor's refund shows sends nothing; a claim whose reading after it failed reopens on the first fresh reading with one claim sent; a claim read unchanged right after it sends no request and no second claim of a grant still listed, then admits as `backed` when a reading within 30 minutes shows the week lower, while one shown lower only after 45 minutes gives no room and a later natural rollover admits ordinarily; a week resetting 9 days ahead, a Fable request on a login whose reading has no Fable window, and one whose Fable window is a 40-minute-old carried reading (the week kept fresh by Opus answers' headers) send nothing until a fresh valid reading; of two hosts on one refund only the claim's host sends; a spent Fable cache rotates to a fresh Fable login. `mods/pool/admission.test.ts` runs a generated claim/dispatch sequence, readings lagging the requests, against an independent model at every dispatch, and every write/read order of two racing reservations; `mods/pool/pace.test.ts` holds the boundaries of 5h, week and a model's own week.
5. **Readings and retry.** `mods/pool/pace.test.ts` generates sequences of readings, omissions, usage endpoint outages and period rollovers for both defined and learned scoped windows. It also advances to each reported retry with refreshed readings, checks a past 5-hour reset, and makes a title wait for ordinary room. In `pool-reset-backed-pace.sim.test.ts`, the directory that claimed a reset closes before a reading shows the refund; the already-running directory on the same host observes it and sends once on that claim.
6. **Named fallback.** `cmd/orchestrate/orchestrate-fallback.sim.test.ts` uses the real pool against the fake Anthropic provider. When its login never reports a Fable window, the planner starts on its admitted fallback; no Fable request goes out. The pool's start and dispatch answers match, and the generated histories verify both the action and time-based holds.

## Gotchas

- Never point a drive at the owner's logins or his login store; a real pool run spends a Claude account.
- The `/pool` pane is the status mod's; with no pool loaded it draws an empty pool, which proves nothing about the pool.
- A failover seen only in the transcript is incomplete: read the provider's requests (which login each went out on).
- A question about a reset in the tray is a regression: the pool claims or waits on its own, and only tells (`Pool reset claimed`, one `OK`).
- Every usage figure the pool shows (pace holds, reset notices and the claim log, `pool_push`, the pane and sidebar) reads as the share used, out of 100% (`70% used of 100%`, `70%/100% used`): a bare `X%`, or a percent of room, left or remaining, is a regression. `cmd/pool/usage-wording.test.ts` holds each kind's text.
