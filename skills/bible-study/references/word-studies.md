## Word Counting

Count occurrences of a specific word within a passage. Symbolic counts are theologically significant.

### Significant Numbers
| Count | Significance | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | Godhead, completeness | Three Hebrew children in furnace |
| 4 | Universality, earth (4 directions) | Four living creatures, four Gospels |
| 7 | Sabbath, divine perfection | Seven churches, seven seals |
| 10 | Law, commandments | "Ten" occurs 10x in Daniel 1 |
| 12 | God's organized people | 12 tribes, 12 apostles |
| 40 | Testing, probation | 40 days/nights, 40 years wandering |
| 70 | Sabbath cycles (7x10) | 70 years captivity, 70 weeks |
| 490 | Probationary time (70x7) | 490 years Daniel 9 |

### Method
1. Identify a word that seems prominent in the passage
2. Count every occurrence (use concordance for precision)
3. Check if the count matches a symbolic number
4. If so, identify what the count connects to thematically

## Root Tracing

Hebrew and Greek words share roots that create hidden connections.

### Example: "Cut Off" and "Covenant"
- כָּרַת *karath* #3772 = "cut off" (Daniel 9:26 — Messiah shall be "cut off")
- בְּרִית *beriyth* #1285 = "covenant" (Daniel 9:27 — "confirm the covenant")
- Both refer to the ancient covenant-cutting ritual: animals split in two, parties walk between halves (Genesis 15:10,17-18)
- Messiah being "cut off" IS the covenant being confirmed — same event, described from two angles

### Example: "Midst" and "Divide"
- חֲצִי *chatsiy* #2677 = "midst" / "half" (Daniel 9:27 — "midst of the week")
- From חָצָה *chatsah* #2673 = "to divide" / "to cut in two"
- The word for "middle of the week" literally means "to cut the week in two"

### Method
1. Identify the Hebrew/Greek word using Strong's
2. Check its root (listed in Strong's concordance)
3. Find other words sharing the same root
4. Ask: does the root connection reveal a theological link?

## Vocabulary Clustering

Words appearing exclusively in paired sections confirm structural relationships.

### Example: Daniel 9:1-2 paired with 9:22-27
| Word | vss. 1-2 | vss. 3-21 (prayer) | vss. 22-27 |
|---|---|---|---|
| "one" | vs. 1 | — | vs. 27 |
| "seventy" | vs. 2 | — | vs. 24 |
| "Daniel" | vs. 2 | — | vs. 22 |
| "understand" | vs. 2 | — | vss. 23, 25 |
| "prophet" | vs. 2 | — | vs. 24 |

These 5+ words appear ONLY in the bookend sections, never in the central prayer.
This proves the bookends are deliberately paired, confirming the chiastic structure.

### Method
1. List all significant words in a suspected pair (A and A')
2. Check if each word appears in the intervening sections (B, C, etc.)
3. Words found ONLY in the paired sections = structural confirmation
4. The more exclusive vocabulary shared, the stronger the confirmation

## Name Analysis

### Hebrew Name Meanings
Biblical names encode theology. Always check meanings.

| Name | Meaning | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| Daniel | "God is my judge" | dan (judge) + El (God) |
| Hananiah | "Yahweh has been gracious" | — |
| Mishael | "Who is what God is?" | — |
| Azariah | "Yahweh has helped" | — |
| Babylon/Babel | "Confusion" / "Gate of God" | Genesis 11:9 — God "confounded" language |

### Alphabetical Order
Names listed in Hebrew alphabetical order signal deliberate arrangement.
Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael, Azariah appear in Hebrew alphabetical order in Daniel 1:6.

### Babylonian vs. Hebrew Names
When characters have both names, the switch between them is structurally significant.
- Daniel vs. Belteshazzar — which name is used when, and why?
- The four friends' Babylonian names (Shadrach, Meshach, Abed-nego) used in ch. 3 — they are known by their Babylonian names during the persecution test

## Greek/Hebraic Distinctions

Different Greek/Hebrew words translated the same way in English carry distinct meanings.

### Example: λέγει vs. λόγον (Revelation 1:8-9)
- λέγει (*legei*) = "saith" — the act of speaking
- λόγον (*logon*) = "word" — the message/content
- Christ IS the Word (Logos) but also *speaks* (legei)
- The structure pairs "saith the Lord God" (B) with "for the word of God" (B')

### Example: σαββατισμός (Hebrews 4:9)
- σαββατισμός *sabbatismos* #4521 = "a Sabbath rest"
- NOT the generic "rest" (κατάπαυσις *katapausis*) used elsewhere in Hebrews 4
- The specific Greek word preserves the Sabbath identity of the rest

### Method
1. When a word seems significant, check the original language
2. Note if the English translation obscures a distinction
3. Check if paired structural positions use different original words
4. The distinction often reveals the theological point

## Strong's Number Usage

### Format
Always cite as #NNNN (e.g., #3772 for כָּרַת *karath*).
Hebrew numbers: #1-#8674. Greek numbers: #1-#5624.

### When to Cite
- Key theological terms in a passage
- Words where the English translation is ambiguous
- Root connections between seemingly unrelated words
- Words that appear in vocabulary clusters

### Example Citation
> "Messiah shall be **cut off** [כָּרַת *karath* #3772]" (Daniel 9:26)

## Verbatim Phrase Tracking

Exact phrase repetitions across books create interpretive connections.

### Example: The Golden Calf Formula
- **Exodus 32:4** — "**These be thy gods, O Israel, which brought thee up out of the land of Egypt**"
- **1 Kings 12:28** — "**Behold thy gods, O Israel, which brought thee up out of the land of Egypt**"
- The near-verbatim repetition is deliberate: Jeroboam's act = a second golden calf apostasy

### Method
1. When a phrase seems familiar, search for exact matches in other books
2. Note any verbal differences (they may be significant)
3. The connection created is always intentional — ask what it implies
